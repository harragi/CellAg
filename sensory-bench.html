Sensory Bench

How taste varies in cellular-agriculture cells when you change media composition, harvest timing, and cooking method. The model translates inputs through three causal chains — Maillard chemistry, lipid oxidation, and umami precursor pools — into eight perceivable flavor dimensions and eight key volatile compounds.

The five dominant flavor levers in cell-ag

What you can actually adjust in a media or process to change how the final product tastes. Listed in rough order of how much they move the needle in a typical cultivated-meat or seafood pipeline.

L Lipid composition
The cell membrane and intracellular lipid droplets reflect what's in the media. Oleic acid (C18:1) → buttery; stearic (C18:0) → tallowy/beefy; linoleic (C18:2) → fresh but oxidation-prone (drives hexanal); DHA/EPA → fishy character that can dominate or come across as oxidative cardboard depending on antioxidants. Fu et al. 2026 (PMID 42041552) showed oleic + transferrin synergistically drives porcine adipogenesis — same lever for human-edible CHO/fish lines.
Fu 2026; Belitz Food Chemistry; Mottram 1998
U Umami precursors
Free glutamate from glutamine pool. IMP (inosine 5'-monophosphate) from RNA breakdown post-harvest — peaks around 24–48h, then degrades to inosine (papery off-note). Yamaguchi 1972's synergy: u(MSG, IMP) ≈ MSG + 1218 · MSG · IMP — a 1:1 mix tastes 8× as umami as either alone. The single most controllable lever for "savory" perception.
Yamaguchi 1972; ChEBI:17202 (IMP), CHEBI:14321 (Glu)
H Heme / iron status
Iron in transferrin → myoglobin in muscle cells. Myoglobin gives the "meaty/blood" character that distinguishes red meat from chicken. Impossible Foods leverages soy leghemoglobin for this. Too much iron → metallic, sometimes liver-like. Cell density and oxygen tension during culture affect myoglobin expression.
Belitz; Impossible Foods technical literature
M Maillard precursors at cooking
Reducing sugars (glucose, glycogen-derived) + free amino acids + heat → pyrazines, furans, thiols. Cysteine specifically drives "meaty" sulfur volatiles. Glycogen content depends on glucose feeding and harvest state. The Maillard reaction is the single biggest delta between raw and cooked perception.
Mottram 1998 (Food Chem); Du 2025 (caail #27)
O Oxidative stress & antioxidant capacity
Lipid oxidation generates aldehydes (hexanal, nonanal, 2,4-decadienal, trans-2-nonenal) that range from desirable "fresh fatty" to off-flavor "cardboard/rancid". Selenium-bound GPx, ascorbate, NAC, and tocopherol all suppress oxidation in vitro and in storage. PUFA-rich (especially DHA/EPA) cells are most vulnerable.
Sun 2023 (caail #26); Chevallier 2020

How the model works
Three coupled chains: (1) Maillard = sugar × AA × heat; (2) Lipid oxidation = PUFA × oxidation_rate × time, where oxidation_rate scales with heat and inverse antioxidant capacity; (3) Umami = glutamate · (1 + 1218 · IMP) from Yamaguchi synergy. Volatile compounds are precursor × heat × oxidation products. Each radar dimension is a normalized blend. This is a teaching/triage model grounded in published food chemistry — not a sensory-panel substitute. Real perception is non-linear, panel-dependent, and culturally variable.

    Try these scenarios

    1. Beef vs chicken: switch the preset, watch beefy axis collapse, fatty/buttery axis stay similar. Iron is the dominant lever.
    2. Lock in fishy: use Salmon preset, push DHA/EPA to 30 µM. Now drop antioxidant to "low" and storage to 7 days. Watch the radar tip from "fishy" toward "oxidized" as the cardboard volatile (trans-2-nonenal) climbs.
    3. The IMP window: hold all else constant, vary aging time from 0 → 72h. Umami synergy peaks around 28-32h and falls past 48h as IMP degrades to inosine.
    4. Maillard differential: use Beef preset, switch cook method through raw → sous-vide → roast → sear. Watch savory axis triple, while fishy and green stay stable (those are oxidation-driven, not heat-driven).
    5. Find the over-aged failure: use Over-aged preset. Verdict should flag oxidation + papery inosine. Now bump antioxidant capacity to "high" — see how much the failure mode recedes.

    What this model does NOT do

    • Real sensory perception. Human flavor is non-linear, threshold-driven, panel-dependent, and shaped by texture/temperature/cultural expectation. Numbers here are physico-chemical predictors, not panel scores.
    • Texture and mouthfeel. Muscle fiber alignment, fat marbling, water-holding capacity — all decisive for perception, all out of scope.
    • Volatile dynamics. Real cooking dynamics involve mass-transfer, evaporation, and Maillard kinetics that are time-dependent and surface-vs-interior dependent. The bench treats cooking as a single multiplier.
    • Off-flavors specific to fermentation byproducts. Yeasty, beany, sulfury notes from precision-fermentation feeds aren't modeled.
    • The ML olfaction frontier. Lee 2023's Principal Odor Map (POM) and POMMix could in principle predict aroma descriptors directly from molecular structure of the predicted volatiles. We name the volatiles here; for the next leg, hand them off to POM-class models.
