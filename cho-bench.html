CHO Media Bench

Interactive bench for varying CHO media components and watching how growth, byproducts, and inhibitor pressure respond. Grounded in published CHO bioprocess kinetics — Monod-style growth with multiplicative inhibition by lactate, ammonia, and osmolarity. The model isn't a substitute for a real iCHO FBA run; it's a fast first-pass to find which knob matters before you spend a wet-lab cycle.

The seven major CHO inhibitors

What slows or kills CHO cultures, in rough order of how often they bite. Threshold values are typical for fed-batch CHO-K1 mAb platforms; specific lines vary.

L Lactate
Inhibits growth above ~40 mM. Y_Lac/Glc > 1.5 mol/mol = Warburg-mode warning.
Glycolytic overflow byproduct. Acidifies the medium (drives base addition → osmolarity rise). Late-fed-batch CHO can reverse to lactate consumption — an desirable mode. Y_Lac/Glc is the headline diagnostic.
Mulukutla 2017, Hartley 2018
N Ammonia (NH₃ / NH₄⁺)
Mild stress at 2–4 mM, significant inhibition >5 mM, glycosylation defects above ~7 mM.
From glutamine catabolism (and to a lesser extent asparagine). Causes intracellular pH stress, mAb sialic-acid-content drop, growth rate decline. Replacing glutamine with glutamate or controlling Gln feed reduces NH₃ accumulation.
Yang & Butler 2002, Chen & Harcum 2006
O Osmolarity / Hyperosmotic stress
Optimal 280–330 mOsm/kg. Mild stress 350–400. Significant >400.
Cumulative effect of base addition (NaOH for pH control), feed concentrates, accumulating substrates and byproducts. High osmolarity slows growth but can increase specific productivity — a tradeoff CHO bioprocess engineers exploit deliberately.
Schmelzer & Miller 2002, Han 2010
G Glucose depletion / starvation
Below ~1 mM, growth halts. Below 5 mM, lactate consumption mode begins.
Not an inhibitor per se but the most common growth-limiting condition. Tightly coupled to lactate dynamics: as Glc drops, q_Lac/q_Glc shifts from ~1.8 (overflow) to negative (consumption).
Mulukutla 2010
Q Glutamine depletion or imbalance
CHO-specific Km ≈ 0.05–0.1 mM. Depletion <0.5 mM throttles growth.
Glutamine is essential for nucleotide biosynthesis and as a TCA-cycle anaplerotic substrate. Asparagine substitutes partially in glutamine-replacement strategies (asparagine + glutamate). Excess glutamine → excess ammonia.
Yang 2014
R Reactive oxygen species
Hard to measure directly; effect proxied via low antioxidant capacity (selenium <10 nM, NAC absent).
Higher mitochondrial flux at low cell density generates ROS faster than the cell can scavenge. Selenium is a cofactor for glutathione peroxidase; supplementation at 30 nM is standard. NAC (N-acetylcysteine) and ascorbate-2-P are common adjuncts.
Chevallier 2020
C CO₂ accumulation (pCO₂)
>100–150 mmHg becomes inhibitory (CHO-K1 dependent).
Becomes a problem at higher cell densities and in larger reactors where stripping is rate-limited. Acts as a growth and productivity inhibitor; difficult to fix at scale without sparging redesign. Not modeled here — needs reactor-physics inputs.
Goudar 2007

How the model works
Specific growth rate μ = μ_max × f(Glc) × f(Gln/Asn) × f(Lac) × f(NH₃) × f(osm) × f(GFs) × viability. Each factor is in [0,1]. Substrate factors are Monod (saturating); inhibitor factors are Ki/(Ki+conc); osmolarity is a Gaussian-ish penalty above the 320 mOsm optimum. Byproducts accumulate as q_X × ∫VCD dt. Y_Lac/Glc shifts from ~1.8 at high glucose to ~0 at depletion (overflow → oxidative metabolism). 1-hour Euler integration. This is a teaching/triage model — not validated against a specific clone. For real predictions run iCHOv1 in COBRApy.

    Reading the bench

    Growth chart

    If the curve plateaus early, you're hitting one of three walls: substrate depletion (check Substrates chart), byproduct inhibition (check Byproducts chart), or osmotic stress (look at the osmolarity trace). The verdict panel auto-flags which one.

    Substrates chart

    Glucose (green) and glutamine (lavender) traces. When glucose hits ~5 mM, the model shifts Y_Lac/Glc downward — visible as the lactate curve flattening or reversing in the byproducts panel.

    Byproducts chart

    Three inhibitors stacked: lactate, ammonia, and osmolarity (right-hand scale, dashed). Lactate near 40 mM = warning band. NH₃ above 5 mM = significant inhibition territory. Osmolarity above 380 = stressed.

    Sensitivity bars

    For each adjustable component, the bar shows the percentage change in final VCD when that component is varied ±20% from the current setting. Long bars = high leverage. Bars pointing in opposite directions for + and − mean you're near a local optimum on that knob (small bilateral deltas). Bars pointing the same direction mean you're below the optimum (more is better) or above (less is better).

    Try these scenarios

    1. Lock in Warburg. Switch to "Glucose-rich" preset. Watch Y_Lac/Glc → ~1.8, lactate hit 40 mM by ~72h, growth flatten. Now drop glucose to 12 mM. Lactate accumulation drops by ~40%; final VCD rises.
    2. Glutamine vs. asparagine substitution. From baseline, drop glutamine to 1 mM and bump asparagine to 10 mM. Watch NH₃ accumulation drop noticeably with only a small μ penalty.
    3. Selenium starvation. Drop selenium to 0. The growth-factor product term hits the ROS-proxy floor; final VCD drops ~25%.
    4. Osmotic walk-off. Bump NaCl background to 200 mM. Even with substrates fine, growth ceiling drops because the osmolarity factor squeezes μ from the start.
    5. Hit the lactate wall hard. Glucose 50 mM, time horizon 240h. Watch the cells stall around hour 120 when lactate crosses 50 mM.

    What this model does not do

    • FBA. The bench uses kinetic Monod-style ODEs, not constraint-based flux balance analysis. For real iCHO-grounded predictions you need COBRApy with iCHOv1 / iCHO2441 SBML.
    • Specific productivity. mAb titer prediction requires a separate sub-model coupled to growth phase and stress. Not in scope.
    • Glycosylation. Glycan profiles depend on UDP-sugar pools, NH₃, Mn²⁺, galactose feeding. Hooks for the GlyGen tool exist in the agent but aren't simulated here.
    • Clone-specific rates. q_X coefficients are set to CHO-K1-mAb-platform medians from literature. Your specific clone differs ±30–60%; calibrate against pilot runs.
    • Feed strategies. All inputs are batch (constant from t=0). Fed-batch dynamics (bolus/continuous feeds) are not implemented.
    • Cell death. Viability decays slowly under stress but doesn't model proper apoptotic kinetics.
